# Validation of the statistics

Generated by `gamma validate` on 2026-11-03, 2000 trials per row, in 74 s.

The population is the 196,470 players of `lobby_layout`, an experiment with no real effect, so the arms are interchangeable and any difference is noise. The estimators are the production ones (`gamma.analyze.estimate_metric`); the data is the heavy-tailed output of the platform's own pipeline, not a textbook normal. A rate near the nominal level, within its own margin (the `±`, a 95% interval for the rate), means the method keeps its promise.

## 2. Power, interval coverage and what CUPED buys

A known relative lift is planted in the treatment arm and the test is run at the sample size the design formula (`gamma.stats.design`) prescribes for 80% power. *Coverage* is how often the 95% interval of the deciding method contains the planted lift (nominal 95%).

| Metric | Planted lift | Players per arm | Power, raw | Power, CUPED | Coverage | Variance with CUPED / without |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## How to read this

- Every trial re-splits the same finite pool of players, so rows are not independent experiments; the `±` shows the Monte Carlo error of each rate, not more.
- A point or so off nominal on the money metrics at 10,000 players per arm is the normal approximation straining on a skewed metric. The percentile bootstrap printed by `gamma analyze` is the cross-check for those.
- For the same reason power on a heavy-tailed continuous metric can land a few points under the planned 80% (`deposit_per_player` does). Plan with margin there.
- Interval coverage of a *relative* lift uses the delta method, which is approximate; it is expected to sit slightly under 95% at small effects.

# Benchmarks

Generated by `gamma bench` on 2026-11-03, ClickHouse 24.8 in Docker on the same machine as the Python process. Wall-clock seconds of a single run in a scratch database on shared cores: read ratios and shapes, not absolute numbers.

## Scaling with the number of players

Three experiments run on the same players. *Marts* builds all three; *analysis* is one experiment (`welcome_bonus`) including a mart rebuild, without and with the bootstrap of the money metrics.

| Players | Bets | Generate | Load | Marts (3 experiments) | Analysis | Analysis + bootstrap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 50,000 | 1.28M | 0.5 s | 1.7 s | 0.7 s | 0.3 s | 0.7 s |
| 100,000 | 2.61M | 1.7 s | 3.0 s | 1.0 s | 0.5 s | 1.1 s |
| 200,000 | 5.19M | 3.3 s | 6.0 s | 2.1 s | 1.0 s | 2.3 s |
| 400,000 | 10.44M | 6.5 s | 20.6 s | 7.2 s | 1.3 s | 4.3 s |

Going from 50,000 to 400,000 players (8x) the marts took 9.7x as long and the analysis 4.8x.

## Where the time of an analysis goes

One analysis of `welcome_bonus` at 400,000 players (4.3 s with the bootstrap, 1.3 s without): rebuilding the mart 0.9 s, reading it into numpy 0.3 s, all estimators (z, Welch, delta, CUPED) 0.02 s, bootstrap of the money metrics 3.1 s. The statistics are cheap; the time is in ClickHouse and in the resampling.
